# How a Family Bakery Cut Order Mistakes in Half with Online Ordering

When Maria and Luis Ortega took over her parents' bakery in Fresno in 2019, special orders came in the way they always had: by phone, on sticky notes, and sometimes on the back of a receipt. Birthday cakes, wedding orders and holiday pies were all tracked on a corkboard behind the counter.

"It worked until it didn't," Maria says. "In December, we'd have sixty special orders on that board. Something always got lost."

## The problem

The bakery, Panadería Ortega, takes around 150 custom orders a month, rising to more than 400 in November and December. Each one had details that could go wrong: the name on a cake, the filling, the pickup time. In a busy month, Maria estimates they were getting about one order in twenty wrong, from a misspelled name to a cake that wasn't ready when the customer arrived.

The phones were another problem. During the holidays, Luis spent much of his morning answering calls instead of baking.

## What they changed

In March 2025, the Ortegas started using our online ordering system for special orders. Customers now choose their cake, filling, decoration and pickup time on the bakery's website and pay a deposit when they order. Each order prints out as a ticket in the kitchen, organized by pickup date.

They kept the phone line for customers who prefer to call, but staff enter those orders into the same system while they're on the phone.

## The results

After their first full holiday season with online ordering:

- **Order mistakes fell from about 1 in 20 to fewer than 1 in 40,** based on the bakery's own count of remade or refunded orders.
- **About 70% of special orders now come in online,** so far fewer calls during the morning rush.
- **No-shows dropped,** because customers now pay a deposit when they order.

"The best part is that I'm back in the kitchen," Luis says. "That's what I wanted to be doing in the first place."

## What's next

This year, the Ortegas plan to add their catering menu to the online system. They're also thinking about letting customers order their regular weekend bread in advance.

"My mom still keeps a pad of paper by the phone, just in case," Maria says. "But she hasn't had to use it much."
