# How to Keep Riding Through the Winter (and Keep Your Bike in Good Shape)

Every November, we hear the same thing at the counter: "I guess that's it for the bike until spring." It doesn't have to be. Plenty of our customers ride all year, and with a few changes to your bike and your routine, winter riding can be surprisingly pleasant. Here's what we recommend.

## Get seen

It gets dark early, and drivers aren't expecting cyclists. Lights are the single most important thing you can add to your bike this time of year.

- **A front light** bright enough to see the road, not just to be seen. For unlit streets or paths, look for at least 400 lumens.
- **A rear light**, ideally one with a daytime flashing mode. Many riders use two in case one runs out of battery.
- **Something reflective** on your ankles or your bag. Moving reflectors catch drivers' eyes better than anything fixed to the frame.

Charge your lights every few days. Batteries drain faster in the cold.

## Think about your tires

Wet leaves, rain, and the occasional frost make the roads more slippery than in summer. You don't necessarily need special tires, but a few things help:

- **Lower your tire pressure slightly** (by about 10%) for more grip. Don't go below the minimum printed on the side of the tire.
- **Wider tires** are more stable on rough or wet roads, if your frame has room for them.
- If you ride on ice regularly, **studded tires** make a real difference. We stock them from 700c down to kids' sizes, and we're happy to fit them for you.

## Add fenders

If you only make one change to your bike for winter, make it fenders. They keep spray off your back, your feet, and the rider behind you. They also keep a lot of grit out of your drivetrain. Full-length fenders work best, but clip-on ones are a good option for bikes without mounting points.

## Dress in layers

You'll warm up quickly once you start riding, so it's better to start a little cold than to overheat and get sweaty.

- A **base layer** that wicks moisture (merino or synthetic, not cotton)
- A **windproof outer layer**. A light rain jacket often works well.
- **Gloves** are a must. Your hands get cold faster than anything else, because they're out in the wind and not moving much.
- A thin **hat or headband** that fits under your helmet
- **Overshoes** or waterproof socks if your commute is long

## Look after your bike

Winter is hard on bikes. Road salt and grit wear out chains, cassettes, and brake pads much faster than in summer.

- **Rinse your bike** with plain water after riding on salty roads, especially the chain, gears, and brakes. Avoid a pressure washer, which pushes water into the bearings.
- **Dry and lube the chain** at least once a week. Use a wet lube in winter; it lasts longer in rain.
- **Check your brake pads** regularly. Wet, gritty conditions can wear through a set of pads in a few weeks.
- If you store your bike in a cold garage and bring it into a warm house, let it dry fully before you put it away.
