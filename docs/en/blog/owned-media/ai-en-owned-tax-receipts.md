# 5 Simple Habits to Make Tax Season Easier for Your Small Business

Every spring, small business owners across the country spend long nights searching through emails, shoeboxes, and bank statements to pull together what their accountant needs. It doesn't have to be that way. A few simple habits during the year can save you hours of stress in March and April, and may even save you money.

Here are five habits we recommend to every small business client.

## 1. Keep business and personal finances separate

If you only do one thing on this list, make it this one. Open a separate bank account and credit card for your business and use them only for business expenses.

Mixing personal and business transactions makes it much harder to see how your business is really doing, and it makes tax preparation slower (and more expensive). It can also cause problems if you're ever audited, because you'll need to show which expenses were for the business.

## 2. Save receipts as you go

Don't wait until the end of the year to deal with receipts. Take a photo of each receipt as soon as you get it, or forward email receipts to a dedicated folder. Most bookkeeping software lets you attach a receipt directly to the transaction.

As a rule of thumb, keep receipts for any business expense, and make a quick note of what it was for, especially for meals and travel. "Lunch with client (Sarah, Riverside Dental) to discuss renewal" is much more useful than a receipt alone.

## 3. Reconcile your accounts every month

Set aside 30 minutes at the end of each month to match your bookkeeping records against your bank and credit card statements. This helps you:

- Catch errors and duplicate charges early
- Spot transactions you forgot to categorize
- Keep an accurate picture of your cash flow

Reconciling monthly is much easier than trying to do twelve months at once in February.

## 4. Set aside money for taxes

If you're self-employed or your business is a pass-through entity, you probably need to make estimated tax payments during the year. A common approach is to move a percentage of every payment you receive, often 25% to 30%, into a separate savings account set aside for taxes.

That way, when estimated payments are due (or when you file), the money is already there. Your accountant can help you work out the right percentage for your situation.

## 5. Track mileage and home office use

If you use your car for business, keep a log of your business trips: the date, where you went, the purpose, and the miles driven. A mileage tracking app can do this automatically.

If you work from home, note the size of your workspace and keep records of household expenses like rent, utilities, and internet. Depending on your situation, you may be able to deduct part of these costs.

## Start now, not in April

The best time to start these habits is today. Even if this year's records are a little messy, getting organized now means next tax season will be much smoother.
