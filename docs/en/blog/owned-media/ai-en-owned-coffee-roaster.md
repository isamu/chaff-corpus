# How to Brew Better Coffee at Home: A Guide from Our Roasters

At Northside Coffee Roasters, the most common question we get at the counter is some version of: "Why doesn't my coffee taste like yours?" The good news is that you don't need expensive equipment to close most of the gap. Small changes in how you brew make a bigger difference than most people expect.

Here are the five things we tell every customer who asks.

## 1. Buy whole beans, and grind just before brewing

Ground coffee loses its aroma quickly. Within about 15 minutes of grinding, much of what makes a coffee taste fresh is already gone. If you can, buy whole beans and grind them right before you brew.

You don't need a high-end grinder to start. A basic burr grinder (around $40–$60) gives a much more even grind than a blade grinder, which helps the coffee taste less bitter.

## 2. Use a scale, not a scoop

Scoops vary a lot depending on the coffee and how full you fill them. A simple kitchen scale takes the guesswork out.

A good starting ratio is **1 gram of coffee for every 16 grams of water**. For one large mug (about 350 ml), that's around 22 grams of coffee. If it tastes too strong, add a little more water next time. If it tastes weak or sour, use a little more coffee.

## 3. Watch your water temperature

Water that's too hot can make coffee taste harsh, and water that's too cool leaves it flat. Aim for **90–96°C (195–205°F)**. If you don't have a thermometer, let the kettle sit for about 30 seconds after it boils.

## 4. Use good water

Coffee is about 98% water, so if your tap water tastes of chlorine, your coffee will too. A simple filter jug makes a noticeable difference in many areas.

## 5. Store beans properly

Keep your beans in an airtight container, somewhere cool and dark, and away from the stove. Avoid the fridge, where beans can pick up moisture and smells. Try to use them within three to four weeks of the roast date printed on the bag.
