# Rainy Day in Harbor Bay? Here's What We'd Do

Let's be honest: it rains here. A lot. Our guests sometimes look out the window at breakfast, see the gray sky, and ask us what on earth they're supposed to do now. Good news: rainy days are some of our favorite days in town. Here's our go-to list, all within a short walk or drive of the inn.

## Start slow at the Tidewater Bakery

No need to rush out the door. Walk down Front Street to the Tidewater Bakery and grab a seat by the window. Their cardamom buns sell out by ten on weekends, so go early. Order a big pot of tea and watch the fishing boats come in. Honestly, there are worse ways to spend a morning.

## Get lost in the bookshop

Second Tide Books is two doors down from the bakery, and it's the kind of shop where you go in for "just a minute" and come out an hour later with three books you didn't know you needed. The back room has a whole wall of local history and old nautical charts. Ask the owner, Ruth, for a recommendation. She has never steered us wrong.

## Visit the maritime museum

It's small, but it punches way above its weight. The shipwreck room is a little spooky in the best way, and kids love climbing into the old lifeboat. Budget about an hour and a half. It's a 10-minute drive or a wet 25-minute walk along the harbor.

## Warm up with a bowl of chowder

By lunchtime, you'll want something hot. Our pick is the Gull & Anchor on the pier. Get the smoked fish chowder and a side of their sourdough. It's not fancy, and that's exactly the point. If there's a wait, put your name down and pop into the gift shop next door.

## Take a (short) storm walk

Hear us out. If the wind isn't too wild, put on a raincoat (we have loaners by the front door) and walk out to the lookout at the end of North Point. Watching the waves crash against the rocks in the rain is something else. Stay on the path and keep well back from the edge.

## End the day by the fire

Come back, dry off, and grab a spot in the lounge. We light the fire every afternoon at four, and there's always a stack of board games and a pot of hot cider. Some of our best evenings have been rainy ones, with guests from three different countries teaching each other card games.

So don't let the forecast put you off. Pack a raincoat, bring a good book, and let Harbor Bay show you its cozy side. We'll keep the fire going.
