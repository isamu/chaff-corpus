# How to Back Up Your Photos (and Why You Should Do It Today)

Your photos are some of the most irreplaceable files you own. Birthdays, vacations, your kids growing up, the dog you had in college — once those images are gone, there's no getting them back. Yet many people keep years of memories on a single phone or laptop, one dropped device or failed hard drive away from losing everything.

The good news is that backing up your photos doesn't have to be complicated. In this guide, we'll walk through the best ways to protect your photo library, from simple cloud options to a more thorough setup.

## The 3-2-1 Rule

Before diving into specific tools, it helps to know the golden rule of backups: **3-2-1**.

- **3** copies of your photos
- **2** different types of storage (for example, your computer and an external drive)
- **1** copy stored off-site (such as in the cloud)

You don't have to follow this perfectly, but the closer you get, the safer your photos will be. Even going from one copy to two is a huge improvement.

## Option 1: Cloud Backup

Cloud services automatically upload your photos to the internet, so they're safe even if your device is lost or broken.

Popular options include:

- **Google Photos**: Works on both Android and iPhone. Offers 15 GB of free storage shared across your Google account.
- **iCloud Photos**: Built into iPhone, iPad, and Mac. Comes with 5 GB free, with paid plans for more.
- **Amazon Photos**: Free unlimited full-resolution photo storage for Amazon Prime members.
- **Dropbox and OneDrive**: General cloud storage that can also back up your camera roll automatically.

**Pros:** Automatic, accessible from anywhere, protected from local disasters like fire or theft.

**Cons:** Ongoing subscription costs for larger libraries, and you're relying on a company to keep your data safe.

## Option 2: External Hard Drive

An external hard drive is a simple, one-time purchase that can hold tens of thousands of photos. A 2 TB drive is affordable and will be more than enough for most people.

To back up, simply connect the drive to your computer and copy your photo folders over. Better yet, use built-in backup software to do it automatically:

- **Mac:** Time Machine
- **Windows:** File History

**Pros:** No monthly fees, fast, and fully under your control.

**Cons:** It only protects you if you remember to back up regularly, and the drive can be lost, stolen, or damaged along with your computer if they're kept in the same place.

## Option 3: A NAS (Network Attached Storage)

For those with very large photo libraries or several family members, a NAS is a small device that sits on your home network and stores files for everyone. Many NAS devices can also back themselves up to the cloud.

This option takes more effort to set up, but it's a powerful solution for photography enthusiasts.

## Putting It All Together

For most people, a great setup looks like this:

1. **Turn on cloud backup** on your phone so new photos are saved automatically.
2. **Keep a copy on your computer** or download your cloud library periodically.
3. **Back up your computer to an external drive** once a month, and store that drive somewhere safe.

That gives you three copies, on two types of storage, with one off-site — the 3-2-1 rule in action.

## Don't Forget to Test Your Backups

A backup is only useful if it works. Every few months, try opening a few random photos from your backup to make sure they're intact. It only takes a minute and can save you from an unpleasant surprise later.

## Final Thoughts

Backing up your photos is one of those tasks that's easy to put off — until it's too late. The best backup plan is the one you'll actually stick with, so start simple. Turn on cloud backup today, and add an external drive when you can. Your future self will thank you.
