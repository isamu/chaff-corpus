# How to Get Your Email Inbox Under Control (and Keep It That Way)

If your inbox has thousands of unread messages and you dread opening it, you're not alone. The good news is that you don't need to read every one of them to get things back under control. Here's a step-by-step approach that works whether you use Gmail, Outlook, or another email service.

## Step 1: Declare "inbox bankruptcy" on old messages

Trying to go through years of old email one message at a time is the main reason people give up. Instead:

1. Search for all emails older than 30 days.
2. Select them all.
3. Move them to an archive folder (in Gmail, click **Archive**; in Outlook, move them to a folder called "Old Inbox").

Nothing is deleted. You can still search for any of these messages later. Your inbox now only holds the last month of mail.

## Step 2: Unsubscribe from what you don't read

Newsletters and promotional emails are usually the biggest source of clutter.

- Search your inbox for the word "unsubscribe."
- Go through the senders and unsubscribe from anything you haven't opened in the last few months.
- Many email apps now show an **Unsubscribe** link at the top of marketing emails, which makes this quicker.

Expect this to take 15–20 minutes the first time. It's worth it.

## Step 3: Set up a few simple filters

Filters (called "rules" in Outlook) automatically sort incoming mail. Start with two or three:

- **Receipts and order confirmations** → a "Receipts" folder
- **Newsletters you want to keep** → a "Reading" folder you check once a week
- **Notifications from tools** (calendar, project management apps) → a "Notifications" folder

Keep it simple. Too many folders and you'll stop checking them.

## Step 4: Use the "two-minute rule" for new email

When you open a new message, decide right away:

- **If it takes less than two minutes to deal with**, do it now: reply, forward, or file it.
- **If it takes longer**, flag it or add it to your to-do list, then archive it.
- **If you don't need it**, delete or archive it.

The goal is to touch each email once, instead of reading it, leaving it, and reading it again tomorrow.

## Step 5: Check email at set times

Constantly checking email makes it feel endless. Try checking it at three set times a day, for example at 9 a.m., after lunch, and an hour before you finish work. Turn off notifications for the rest of the day if your job allows it.

## Keeping it under control

A clean inbox doesn't stay clean on its own. Once a week, take ten minutes to:

- Archive anything you've finished with
- Unsubscribe from any new newsletters you're not reading
- Check your flagged messages and clear the ones you've handled

That's all it takes. You may never hit "inbox zero," and that's fine. The goal is an inbox that shows you what needs your attention, not one that buries it.
