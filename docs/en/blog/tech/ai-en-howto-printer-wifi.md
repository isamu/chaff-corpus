# How to get your home printer back on Wi-Fi (without losing your mind)

You hit print. Nothing happens. Your laptop says the printer is "offline," even though it's sitting right there with its little green light on. Sound familiar?

Nine times out of ten, the printer has just dropped off your Wi-Fi. Here's how to get it back, roughly in order of "try this first."

## 1. Turn everything off and on again

I know. But seriously, do it in this order:

1. Turn off the printer.
2. Unplug your router for 30 seconds, then plug it back in.
3. Wait until the router's lights settle down (a couple of minutes).
4. Turn the printer back on.

This fixes it a surprising amount of the time, especially if your internet provider recently pushed an update to the router.

## 2. Check that the printer is on the right network

A lot of routers broadcast two networks, one ending in "2.4G" and one in "5G." Most home printers only work on the 2.4 GHz one. If your laptop is on the 5G network and the printer is on 2.4G, that's usually fine, but if the printer somehow got set up on a guest network, your laptop won't see it.

On the printer's screen (or its settings page), look for something like **Network** or **Wi-Fi Settings** and check which network it's connected to.

## 3. Run the printer's Wi-Fi setup again

If it's not connected at all, go through the setup again:

- On printers with a screen, look for **Wireless Setup Wizard** and pick your network.
- On printers without a screen, there's usually a Wi-Fi button. Hold it until the light blinks, then press the **WPS** button on your router within two minutes.

Changed your Wi-Fi password recently? That's almost certainly the problem. The printer is still trying the old one.

## 4. Remove and re-add the printer on your computer

Sometimes the printer is fine and it's your computer that's confused.

- **Windows:** Settings → Bluetooth & devices → Printers & scanners → click your printer → Remove. Then click **Add device**.
- **Mac:** System Settings → Printers & Scanners → select the printer → click the minus button. Then click **Add Printer**.

## 5. Give the printer a fixed address (if it keeps happening)

If your printer drops off every few weeks, your router may be giving it a new address each time. You can fix this in your router's settings by "reserving" an IP address for the printer. Every router does this a bit differently, so search for your router model plus "DHCP reservation." It sounds technical, but it's usually just a couple of clicks.

## Still not working?

At that point, check whether the printer maker has a free app (most do). They often have a "fix connection" tool that sorts out things the menus don't show you. And if all else fails, a USB cable still works perfectly well. No shame in it.
