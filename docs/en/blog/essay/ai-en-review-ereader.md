# Two years with a basic e-reader

I bought the cheapest e-reader from one of the big brands two years ago, for $99. It's the one without warm light or waterproofing. I was sure I'd end up wanting the more expensive model. I haven't, so here's what it's like to live with.

## Reading

The screen is the reason to own one, and it's good. It looks like paper, not a screen, and I can read for an hour in bed without my eyes getting tired the way they do on my phone. The front light is white and a little cold. In a dark bedroom I keep it at its lowest setting, and it's fine.

I read about 25 books a year, and most of them are now on this. Novels work best. Anything with maps, charts or photos is a pain: images are small, gray, and slow to zoom. I still buy cookbooks and travel guides on paper.

## Battery

I charge it about once every three weeks. I've never had it die on me, including on a ten-day trip where I forgot the cable.

## The store

You're tied to the brand's store, and it pushes its own deals hard. The lock screen shows ads unless you pay $20 to remove them. I paid on the second day. If you know that going in, it's not a big deal.

Borrowing from my local library works through an app, and it's easy once it's set up. The first time took me twenty minutes to work out.

## What's annoying

- Page turns are slightly slow. You get used to it.
- There are no physical buttons, so in winter, with gloves, I can't turn pages.
- The case I bought separately added $25 and some weight. Without a case, the screen scratched within a month.

## Would I buy it again

Yes. The more expensive models have a warmer light, a bigger screen and water resistance. Of those, I've only missed water resistance, once, at the beach. For $99, plus $20 for no ads, this has been one of the best things I've bought.
