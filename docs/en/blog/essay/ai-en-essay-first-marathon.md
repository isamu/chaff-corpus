# Mile 20

I ran my first marathon on Sunday. I'm writing this on Tuesday, and I still walk down stairs sideways.

A lot of people told me that the race starts at mile 20. I nodded every time, the way you nod at advice you don't really believe. I'd done a 20-mile training run. It was long and boring, and I was fine. How different could six more miles be?

Pretty different, it turns out.

The first half went better than I'd hoped. It was cool and overcast, the crowds were loud, and I kept having to remind myself to slow down. Somewhere around mile 8, a woman in a tutu passed me, and I let her go, which I'm still a little proud of. At the halfway mark I was two minutes ahead of my plan and feeling great, which, again, everyone had warned me about.

Around mile 18, my legs started to feel heavy. Not painful, just heavy, like I was running in jeans. By mile 20, something in my left calf started twitching, and every time my foot hit the ground I wondered whether this would be the step where it cramped.

Mile 21 was the worst. There's a long, slow hill on the course there, with no crowd, just a highway overpass and some orange cones. I walked part of it. I'd promised myself I wouldn't walk, and I walked anyway, and the strange thing is that I didn't feel bad about it. I just thought: okay, this is what it is now.

What got me moving again was a guy holding a sign that said "YOU PAID FOR THIS." I laughed out loud, which hurt, and then I started jogging again because it seemed silly not to.

The last two miles are a blur. I remember the noise getting louder and seeing the finish arch and not quite believing it was the real one. My time was 4:41. My goal was 4:30. I don't care at all.

I don't know if I'll do another one. Today I'm leaning toward no. My friend who's run six of them says that's what everyone says on Tuesday.
