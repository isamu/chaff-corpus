# In defense of working from cafés (sort of)

Every few months someone on the internet writes a thread about how people who work from cafés are ruining cafés. Hogging tables. Nursing one latte for four hours. Taking calls. And look, some of that is fair. But I've been doing it for three years, a couple of days a week, and I want to put in a word for us.

First, why bother? I have a desk at home. It's a nice desk. But by about Wednesday, the apartment starts to feel like a small room I've been locked in, and my brain goes flat. Moving to a café doesn't make me more productive, exactly. It makes me less bored, which ends up being the same thing.

There's something about background noise that helps, too. Not loud music, not someone's phone call, but the general clatter of cups and low talking. At home I'll find a reason to get up every ten minutes. At the café I'll sit and write for an hour and look up surprised.

Okay, now the etiquette, because I do think there are rules:

- **Buy something every couple of hours.** Not just one coffee for the whole day. A pastry, a second drink, lunch if they do it. You're renting the table.
- **No calls.** If I have a call, I go home or I step outside. Nobody wants to hear your quarterly numbers.
- **Give up the big table.** If I'm alone, I take the small one by the wall, never the six-seater.
- **Leave during the lunch rush** if it's a small place and people are hovering.

I've also learned which cafés actually like it. Some put up "no laptops on weekends" signs, which seems totally reasonable to me. Others clearly want people to stay. They've got outlets everywhere and the Wi-Fi password is on a chalkboard. Those are my places.

And honestly? I've ended up knowing the staff at my regular spot pretty well. The barista knows my order, I know about her ceramics class, and when I didn't come in for two weeks last spring she asked if I'd been sick. That's more than I can say for my home desk.

So yeah. I hear the complaints. Some of us deserve them. But a few of us are trying.
