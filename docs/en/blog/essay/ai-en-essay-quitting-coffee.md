# What Happened When I Quit Coffee for 30 Days

For as long as I can remember, my mornings have started the same way: alarm, kitchen, coffee. Usually two cups before I'd said a word to anyone, and a third around 2 p.m. to get through the afternoon slump. I never thought much about it. Coffee was just part of the day, like brushing my teeth.

Then, last month, I decided to see what life looked like without it.

## Why I Did It

There was no dramatic reason. I'd been sleeping badly for a while, waking up at 3 a.m. with my mind racing, and a friend mentioned that her sleep improved after she cut back on caffeine. I figured 30 days was long enough to notice a difference and short enough that I could survive it.

## The First Week Was Hard

I'd read about caffeine withdrawal, but I wasn't prepared for it. By the afternoon of day one, I had a dull headache that sat right behind my eyes. On day two, it was worse. I was tired in a heavy, foggy way that made simple emails feel like a lot of work.

I also hadn't realized how much of my routine was built around coffee. The walk to the café near my office, the break I took at 2 p.m., the cup I held during morning meetings. Without it, I didn't quite know what to do with my hands.

By day five, the headaches were gone. By the end of the first week, the fog had started to lift.

## What Changed

The biggest surprise was my energy. I expected to feel tired all month. Instead, after the first week, my energy became more even. I no longer had the big morning high followed by the afternoon crash. I wasn't buzzing, but I wasn't dragging either.

My sleep improved too, though not right away. In the third week, I realized I hadn't woken up at 3 a.m. in several days. I was falling asleep faster and waking up feeling more rested.

I also saved money. Between the café coffees and the beans I bought for home, I'd been spending around $90 a month without thinking about it.

## What I Missed

I won't pretend it was all positive. I missed the taste. I missed the ritual of making a cup in the morning, and I missed the excuse to step away from my desk for a few minutes. Herbal tea helped with the ritual, but it never felt quite the same.

## Where I've Landed

The 30 days ended last week, and I've gone back to coffee, but differently. I now have one cup in the morning, and none after noon. It's enough to enjoy it without it running my day.

The experiment taught me something I didn't expect: I'd been using coffee to fix a problem that coffee was partly causing. Sometimes the only way to see a habit clearly is to step away from it for a while.
