# Proposal: Moving Project Tracking from Spreadsheets to a Shared Project Management Tool

## Summary

Our team of nine currently tracks projects in a set of shared spreadsheets. This approach served us well when the team was smaller, but it has become difficult to keep up to date and hard to get a clear picture of who is working on what. This proposal recommends moving to a dedicated project management tool, outlines the expected benefits and costs, and suggests a phased rollout over six weeks.

## Current Situation

Today, each project has its own spreadsheet, and a master sheet lists all active projects. Team members update their tasks manually, and the team lead consolidates status before the weekly meeting.

This setup has several drawbacks:

- **Information gets out of date.** Updates depend on people remembering to edit the sheet, and status is often a few days behind.
- **Ownership is unclear.** It is not always obvious who is responsible for a task, especially when tasks are handed off.
- **Visibility is limited.** There is no easy way to see everyone's workload or upcoming deadlines across projects.
- **Collaboration is scattered.** Discussions about tasks happen in email and chat, separate from the task itself, so context gets lost.
- **Consolidation takes time.** Preparing for the weekly status meeting takes the team lead roughly two hours each week.

## Proposed Solution

We propose adopting a shared project management tool such as Asana, Trello, or Jira. All three offer the core features we need:

- Task assignment with owners and due dates
- Board and timeline views across projects
- Comments and file attachments on each task
- Notifications when tasks change or deadlines approach
- Simple reporting on progress and workload

We recommend a short evaluation of two or three options before making a final choice, with the team's input on usability.

## Expected Benefits

- **Up-to-date status:** Changes are visible to everyone immediately, reducing the need for manual consolidation.
- **Clear accountability:** Every task has a single owner and a due date.
- **Better planning:** Workload and timeline views make it easier to spot conflicts and bottlenecks early.
- **Less time in status meetings:** With status visible in the tool, the weekly meeting can focus on decisions and blockers rather than updates.
- **Centralized context:** Discussions and files live with the task, making handoffs smoother.

## Costs

Most tools in this category cost between $10 and $25 per user per month. For a team of nine, that comes to approximately $1,080 to $2,700 per year. Several options also offer a free tier that may cover our basic needs.

Beyond licensing, the main cost is time: we estimate about two hours per person for initial training and setup, plus time for the team lead to configure projects and templates.

## Implementation Plan

| Week | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| 1–2 | Evaluate two or three tools with a small group; select one |
| 3 | Set up the workspace, project templates, and permissions |
| 4 | Train the team; migrate active projects |
| 5–6 | Run the new tool alongside spreadsheets; gather feedback |
| After week 6 | Retire spreadsheets for active projects |

## Risks and Mitigation

- **Adoption:** Team members may continue using spreadsheets out of habit. We will set a clear cutover date and keep the setup simple at first.
- **Migration effort:** Moving existing projects takes time. We will migrate only active projects and archive completed ones as they are.
- **Tool fit:** A tool may not match how we work. The evaluation period and pilot weeks are designed to surface this early.

## Recommendation

We recommend approving a six-week rollout of a shared project management tool, starting with a two-week evaluation. This change would give the team a clearer, more reliable view of our work and free up time currently spent maintaining spreadsheets. We would be happy to discuss any questions or adjustments to this plan.
