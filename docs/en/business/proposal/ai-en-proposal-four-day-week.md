# Proposal: a four-day week pilot for the design team

## What I'm asking for

I'd like to run a three-month pilot where our 12-person design team works four days a week instead of five, at the same pay, starting in January. Everyone would work Monday to Thursday, with Fridays off. At the end of the pilot we'd look at what happened to our output, our deadlines and how people are doing, and decide together whether to keep it, change it or drop it.

## Why I think it's worth trying

Our work comes in bursts. Most of the hard thinking happens in a few focused stretches each week, and a lot of Friday tends to go to small fixes, catching up on messages and meetings that could probably have been an email. I don't think we'd lose much by giving that day back, especially if we're deliberate about how we use the other four.

There's also a retention angle. We lost two senior designers this year, and in both exit conversations burnout came up. Hiring and onboarding a senior designer takes us around four months before they're fully up to speed. If a shorter week helps us keep people, it pays for itself pretty quickly.

And honestly, other studios are already doing this. A couple of candidates we interviewed last spring asked about it directly.

## How it would work

- **Same pay, same goals.** This isn't a pay cut or a reduction in what we're expected to deliver.
- **Fridays off for everyone**, so we're not constantly working around each other's schedules. If a client deadline really needs Friday coverage, we'll rotate one person and they'll take another day off that week.
- **Fewer and shorter meetings.** We'd cut our standing meetings by about a third and cap most at 30 minutes. I'll go through the calendar with the team before January.
- **Client communication stays the same.** Account managers keep their normal schedule, so clients won't see a gap on Fridays. We'll just be clear about when design reviews can happen.

## How we'd know if it's working

I'd want to measure this properly rather than go on gut feel. Before the pilot starts, we'll take a baseline from the last quarter, and then track the same things during the pilot:

- Projects delivered on time
- Revision rounds per project (a rough proxy for quality)
- Client satisfaction scores, which we already collect
- A short monthly team survey on workload and stress
- Hours people actually work, so we can see if Friday work is creeping back in

If on-time delivery or client scores drop noticeably and don't recover by the second month, we'll stop early and go back to five days.

## What could go wrong

The biggest risk is that the work just gets squeezed into four longer days and people end up more tired, not less. That's why I want to track hours and run the survey, and why the meeting cuts matter. Another risk is that a rush project lands during the pilot and Fridays quietly disappear. If that happens, I'd rather pause the pilot for that week and say so than pretend it's still running.

## Cost

There's no direct cost. The real cost is the risk of slower delivery, and the three-month limit with clear stop conditions is how I'd keep that small.
