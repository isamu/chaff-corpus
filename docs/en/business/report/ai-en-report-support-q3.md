# Q3 Customer Support Report

## Executive Summary

Customer support handled 4,820 tickets in Q3, a 12% increase over Q2. Despite the higher volume, the team significantly improved its responsiveness, cutting median first response time from 4.5 hours to 3.1 hours. Customer satisfaction (CSAT) dipped slightly, from 89% to 87%, driven largely by a spike in login issues following the September app update.

## Key Metrics

| Metric | Q2 | Q3 | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Total tickets | 4,304 | 4,820 | +12% |
| Median first response time | 4.5 hours | 3.1 hours | −31% |
| CSAT | 89% | 87% | −2 pts |

## Ticket Volume

Ticket volume rose steadily through the quarter, with the largest increase in September. Much of this growth can be attributed to the September app update, which generated a wave of login-related tickets. Excluding those tickets, volume grew roughly in line with our active user base.

## Response Time

The improvement in first response time is one of the quarter's clear wins. Several factors contributed:

- **New triage process:** Tickets are now tagged and routed automatically by category, reducing the time agents spend sorting the queue.
- **Expanded coverage:** We added an evening shift in July, which shortened response times for tickets submitted after 5 p.m.
- **Saved replies:** The team built a library of templates for common questions, allowing faster initial responses.

## Customer Satisfaction

CSAT declined by two points to 87%. The drop was concentrated in September and closely tracks the rise in login issues. Customers who contacted us about login problems rated their experience lower on average, often citing frustration with the issue itself rather than with the support they received.

## Top Issue: Login Problems

Login problems were the single largest category of tickets in Q3, accounting for 22% of total volume (approximately 1,060 tickets). Most were reported after the September app update and involved users being signed out unexpectedly or being unable to sign in with saved credentials.

The support team worked closely with engineering to identify the root cause, and a fix was released in early October. We have already seen login-related tickets drop since the fix went out.

## Recommendations

1. **Involve support in release planning.** Giving the support team advance notice of major updates would allow us to prepare help articles and saved replies before issues reach customers.
2. **Add a status banner for known issues.** An in-app or help-center banner could reduce duplicate tickets during incidents.
3. **Monitor CSAT for Q4.** With the login fix in place, we expect CSAT to recover. We will track it closely and report back next quarter.

## Conclusion

Q3 was a busy quarter for support. The team handled a significant increase in volume while delivering faster responses, which reflects the impact of the process improvements made this year. The login issue was a setback for customer satisfaction, but it also highlighted the value of closer coordination between support and engineering heading into Q4.
