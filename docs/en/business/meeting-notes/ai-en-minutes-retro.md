# Project Retrospective: Website Relaunch

**Date:** November 6, 2026
**Facilitator:** [Name]
**Participants:** [Names]

## Purpose

To reflect on the website relaunch project, which went live on October 28, and identify what worked well, what didn't, and what we want to do differently next time.

## What Went Well

- **Clear ownership of each page.** Assigning one content owner per section meant fewer conflicting edits and faster approvals.
- **Early stakeholder previews.** Sharing the staging site with leadership three weeks before launch surfaced most of the major feedback early.
- **Launch day itself.** The cutover went smoothly, with no downtime and only minor issues reported in the first 48 hours.
- **Collaboration between design and development.** Daily check-ins during the build phase helped resolve questions quickly.

## What Didn't Go Well

- **Content was late.** About 40% of page copy arrived after the deadline, which compressed the time available for review and testing.
- **Scope creep.** Several features (the events calendar and the partner directory) were added mid-project without adjusting the timeline.
- **Testing on mobile devices was rushed.** A few layout issues on smaller Android phones were only found after launch.
- **Decision-making was sometimes slow.** Some design decisions waited more than a week for approval.

## What We'll Do Differently

1. Set content deadlines two weeks earlier than the build schedule requires, and track them in the project plan.
2. Introduce a simple change request process: any new feature needs an agreed impact on the timeline before it's accepted.
3. Build a device testing checklist and start mobile testing in parallel with development, not at the end.
4. Agree on a maximum turnaround time (three working days) for design approvals.
